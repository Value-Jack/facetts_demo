Weaving Prosody Priors into Speech :

A MultiModal Retrieval-Augmented Generation Framework for Face-Driven Text-To-Speech

Abstract

Given a text and a face image, face-driven text-to-speech (TTS) aims to synthesize speech whose linguistic content follows the text and whose speaker characteristics are compatible with the face. However, existing methods often produce monotonous speech because utterance-level rhythm and intonation cannot be uniquely inferred from a static image. We propose PRFTTS (Prosody Retrieval for Face-driven Text-to-Speech), a multimodal retrieval-augmented framework for expressive face-driven speech synthesis. Instead of directly predicting prosody from the face, PRFTTS separates speaker identity from utterance-level prosody. Speaker Prototype Alignment maps the face representation into a stable speaker embedding space and provides identity conditioning to the acoustic decoder. In parallel, a face-and-text retrieval module retrieves natural pitch and energy trajectories from an external database. A progressive fusion strategy then introduces the retrieved prosody into the speech-token language model, enabling diverse prosodic patterns while preserving speaker consistency. Experiments on LRS3 demonstrate competitive improvements in speaker consistency, face--voice compatibility, speech naturalness, and prosodic expressiveness over existing face-driven TTS systems.
